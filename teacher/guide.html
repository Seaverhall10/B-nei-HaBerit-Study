B’nei Haberit Facilitator Guide

This guide is generated from the same twelve-week source as the participant site. It is intentionally excluded from the public website deployment.

Week 1

Creation, Eden, and the Human Calling

Spine: Genesis 1:26-28

Big idea: Yahweh alone creates humanity in his image to represent his rule on Earth. The Eden rebel attacks that vocation; Jesus fulfills and restores it as the last Adam.

Facilitator moves

  • Open with image as vocation, not merely ontology.
  • Read 1:26-28 slowly; ask who we are meant to represent.
  • Use Job 38:4-7 for heavenly family watching creation.
  • Guard against treating Eden as a mere orchard story.
  • Point ahead to Jesus as last Adam without skipping Genesis.

What participants should notice

  • Note who is speaking in Genesis 1:26 and what image/likeness language is doing.
  • Trace the human task in 1:28 and 2:15: rule, fill, work, keep.
  • Watch the serpent invert Yahweh word in 3:1-5.
  • Mark the first gospel hint in 3:15 and the exile from sacred space in 3:22-24.
  • Connect image-bearing with Psalm 8 and last-Adam language in Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15.

Question: What does Genesis say humans are created to be and do?

Guardrail: Do not reduce image of God to a list of human faculties while ignoring royal-priestly vocation.

Response: Blessed are you, Yahweh; you made us to walk with you. Restore our calling in Messiah.

Week 2

Seed Line, Watchers, and Flood

Spine: Genesis 6:1-8

Big idea: Rebellion spreads from Eden through bloodshed and through transgression of the heavenly/earthly boundary. Yahweh judges the world by flood and preserves a seed through Noah.

Facilitator moves

  • Honor the group request: spend real time on Watchers, Nephilim, and later giant clans without turning class into folklore hour.
  • Read Genesis 6:1-8 aloud twice; then Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4-5.
  • Distinguish biblical data from later speculation.
  • Keep the through-line: Yahweh preserves a seed.
  • Land on 1 Peter 3: Christ is the greater ark.

What participants should notice

  • Track two lines after Eden: violence (Cain) and the named seed that still calls on Yahweh.
  • Read Genesis 6:1-4 with Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4: who crossed the boundary?
  • Ask what Nephilim language is doing (and what it is not).
  • Notice why Noah is righteous in his generation and how the ark is rescue through judgment.
  • Connect 1 Peter 3:18-22: Jesus proclaims victory; baptism corresponds to being brought safely through water.
  • Mark the Noahic covenant in 9:8-17: Yahweh binds himself to preserve the earth for his purposes.

Question: How does rebellion spread, and what does Yahweh preserve?

Guardrail: Do not let giant-clan curiosity eclipse the gospel: flood is about Yahweh justice and mercy, not monster lore.

Response: Yahweh, judge evil and keep a remnant. Hide us in Messiah, our ark.

Week 3

Babel and the Nations

Spine: Genesis 11:1-9; Deuteronomy 32:8-9

Big idea: At Babel Yahweh divides the nations. Deuteronomy 32 describes an allotment under lesser powers; Yahweh keeps Israel as his portion and begins reclaiming the nations through Abraham.

Facilitator moves

  • Connect Babel to Pentecost later without dumping week 11.
  • Keep Psalm 82 as courtroom, not trivia.
  • Emphasize Abraham as the start of reclaiming nations.

What participants should notice

  • Map Genesis 10 as the table of nations, then 11 as the reason for scattering.
  • Read Deuteronomy 32:8-9 (compare DSS/LXX sons of God language if you have a note).
  • Hear Psalm 82: corrupt rulers of the nations will be judged.
  • Watch Abraham called out so that all families of the earth will be blessed.
  • Acts 17: God appointed times and boundaries so people might seek him.

Question: What happened to the nations at Babel, and how does Yahweh begin to get them back?

Guardrail: Do not treat other nations as subhuman; the point is spiritual allotment and Yahweh plan to bless all families.

Response: Gather the nations, O God, into the blessing of Abraham.

Week 4

Covenant and the Family of Faith

Spine: Genesis 15:1-6; Genesis 17:1-8

Big idea: Yahweh cuts covenant with Abraham, promising land, seed, and blessing. Faith is counted as righteousness; the family of faith will bless the nations.

Facilitator moves

  • Draw a simple covenant chart: parties, promises, sign.
  • Read Romans 4 after Genesis 15.
  • Keep Isaac as seed, not as a morality play only.

What participants should notice

  • List the promises in Genesis 12, 15, and 17.
  • Note who walks the pieces in 15: Yahweh binds himself.
  • Watch the test of the seed in Genesis 22.
  • Hear Paul: Abraham believed God.
  • Ask how Gentile believers are grafted into this family without becoming a different people of God.

Question: What does covenant with Abraham actually promise, and how is it received?

Guardrail: Do not flatten covenant into a generic self-help contract.

Response: Count us in Abraham family by faith in the promised Seed.

Week 5

Exodus and Sinai

Spine: Exodus 19:4-6

Big idea: Yahweh redeems Israel from slavery, judges the gods of Egypt, and brings a people to Sinai to be a kingdom of priests and a holy nation.

Facilitator moves

  • Keep Passover as rescue unto vocation.
  • Do not skip the calf: holiness is gift and demand.
  • Preview tabernacle next week.

What participants should notice

  • Name the contest with Pharaoh and with Egypt gods.
  • Trace blood, lamb, and firstborn in Passover.
  • Hear 19:4-6 before the Ten Words.
  • Watch the golden calf as covenant breach and Moses intercession.
  • Connect 1 Peter 2: the priestly people now includes the nations in Messiah.

Question: Why does Yahweh save Israel, and what kind of people does Sinai form?

Guardrail: Do not treat Torah as a ladder to earn rescue; rescue comes first.

Response: You brought us out on eagles wings. Make us holy as you are holy.

Week 6

Presence and Holiness

Spine: Exodus 40:34-38; Leviticus 16

Big idea: Yahweh dwells in the midst of Israel. Sacred space, priesthood, and Day of Atonement teach how a holy God can live with an unholy people.

Facilitator moves

  • Sketch the courts; keep it simple.
  • Connect sacred space back to Eden and forward to John 1:14.
  • Guardrail: holiness is not fussiness; it is fitness for presence.

What participants should notice

  • Eden echoes: east, cherubim, gold, gardens, lamp.
  • Trace who may approach and how.
  • Watch two goats on the Day of Atonement.
  • Hear be holy for I am holy in ordinary neighbor-love (Lev 19).
  • Hebrews 9: Messiah enters the true tent with his own blood.

Question: How can Yahweh live among a people without destroying them?

Guardrail: Do not treat Leviticus as primitive leftovers. It is about presence.

Response: Dwell among us, Holy One. Cleanse us that we may draw near.

Week 7

David and the Faithful King

Spine: 2 Samuel 7:8-16

Big idea: Yahweh chooses a king after his heart and covenants with David: an everlasting throne. The faithful king will embody Israel vocation and crush the rebel powers.

Facilitator moves

  • Keep 2 Samuel 7 as the hub.
  • Let the psalms preach.
  • Preview a greater Son of David.

What participants should notice

  • Contrast Saul and David as kinds of rule.
  • List the Davidic promises in 2 Samuel 7.
  • Read Psalm 2 and 110 as royal/messianic.
  • Isaiah 11: Spirit-anointed branch who judges righteously.
  • Ask how giant-slaying (Goliath) echoes seed vs serpent without forcing every detail.

Question: What kind of king does Yahweh promise, and why does Israel need one?

Guardrail: Do not baptize modern politics as the Davidic throne.

Response: Let the Son of David reign; teach us to love his rule.

Week 8

Exile and Prophetic Hope

Spine: Jeremiah 31:31-34; Ezekiel 36:24-28

Big idea: Because of covenant unfaithfulness Israel is expelled from the land, repeating Eden exile. The prophets promise a new covenant, a new heart, and a return under Yahweh own rule.

Facilitator moves

  • Link exile to Genesis 3 geographically and theologically.
  • Do not end in despair; read the hope texts aloud.
  • Daniel 7 sets up Jesus favorite title.

What participants should notice

  • Name causes of exile in 2 Kings 17.
  • New covenant: law written on the heart.
  • Water, Spirit, new heart in Ezekiel 36.
  • Dry bones: resurrection of a people.
  • Daniel 7: one like a son of man receives the kingdom.

Question: What does exile mean, and what hope does Yahweh speak into it?

Guardrail: Do not treat exile as only ancient geopolitics. It is sacred-space loss.

Response: Give us a new heart. Bring us home.

Week 9

Jesus, the Faithful Son and Last Adam

Spine: Luke 3:21-4:13; Romans 5:18-19

Big idea: Jesus is Israel in one person and the last Adam: faithful Son where Adam and Israel failed. He recaptures sacred vocation and withstands the rebel.

Facilitator moves

  • Read the temptation beside Genesis 3.
  • Keep fully God and fully human without a philosophy lecture.
  • Set up week 10: this faithful life goes to a cross.

What participants should notice

  • Word became flesh and tabernacled among us.
  • Genealogy and baptism: beloved Son.
  • Wilderness: three tests vs Eden and vs Israel in the desert.
  • Last Adam in Romans 5.
  • Hebrews 2: he shares flesh and blood to destroy the one who has the power of death.

Question: How is Jesus both true human and the one who succeeds where Adam and Israel failed?

Guardrail: Do not treat Jesus as a mere moral example. He is the last Adam who represents us.

Response: Faithful Son, live in us what we could not live alone.

Week 10

Cross and Resurrection

Spine: 1 Corinthians 15:3-8; Colossians 2:13-15

Big idea: At the cross Jesus dies for sin and disarms the powers. In the resurrection he is firstfruits of new creation and public victory over death.

Facilitator moves

  • Hold penal, victorious, and new-creation notes together.
  • Do not skip resurrection; Friday is not the end.
  • Keep it pastoral: this is for sinners in the room.

What participants should notice

  • Servant song: bearing, substitution, many accounted righteous.
  • Temple veil / centurion confession.
  • Mary at the tomb: new-garden echoes.
  • Paul creed: died, buried, raised, appeared.
  • Colossians 2: powers stripped and publicly shamed.

Question: What happened at the cross and the empty tomb for us and for the powers?

Guardrail: Do not reduce the cross to inspirational suffering or the resurrection to a metaphor.

Response: Lamb who was slain, you are worthy. Raise us with you.

Week 11

Spirit, Assembly, and the Nations Reclaimed

Spine: Acts 2:1-12; Ephesians 3:4-10

Big idea: The Spirit is poured out; Babel is reversed; the assembly becomes the place where the nations are reclaimed as Yahweh inheritance in Messiah.

Facilitator moves

  • Name Babel reversal explicitly.
  • Keep church as assembly, not a building.
  • Mission is reclaiming allotted nations, not hobby.

What participants should notice

  • Pentecost languages vs Babel scattering.
  • Peter: this Jesus, Lord and Messiah.
  • Cornelius: Gentiles receive the Spirit.
  • Ephesians 2: one new humanity, dividing wall down.
  • Revelation 7: every nation before the throne.

Question: What does the Spirit do to Yahweh people and to the nations?

Guardrail: Do not treat the Spirit as private energy detached from the nations story.

Response: Come, Holy Spirit. Make us one people for the sake of the many.

Week 12

Serpent Judged, Eden Restored, New Earth

Spine: Revelation 21:1-5; Revelation 22:1-5

Big idea: The rebel is judged, death dies, and Eden is more than restored: a garden-city where God dwells with humanity on a new earth. Image-bearers finally rule as they were made to.

Facilitator moves

  • Close the loop to week 1 vocation.
  • Keep hope embodied: new earth, not escape.
  • Send them out with a charge, not only a chart.

What participants should notice

  • Creation groaning for the revealing of the children of God.
  • Last enemy: death. All enemies under his feet.
  • Serpent/devil thrown down; death and Hades judged.
  • New heaven and new earth; God with them.
  • Tree of life, river, face of God, they will reign forever.

Question: How does the story end, and what are humans for in the end?

Guardrail: Do not swap the Bible ending for a disembodied heaven-only script.

Response: Even so, come Lord Jesus. Restore all things.
