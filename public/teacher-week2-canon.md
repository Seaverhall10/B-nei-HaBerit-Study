## 2. Genesis 3:15 — zera, hu, he

Read it. “I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed. He will crush your head. You will strike his heel.”

Hebrew זֶרַע (zera, seed) is a masculine noun here. The pronoun is הוּא (hu): he. The verb of crushing is masculine. The heel is his: the suffix on the striking is masculine. English “offspring” is thinner. It can sound like a they, a line, a vague biological future. The Hebrew is a he. A person. Seed against seed, the rebel’s line against the promised one. That is why Week 1 called 3:15 load-bearing, and why Week 2 is not a monster tour. The flood is a strike on the line. Noah is a seed kept.

Circulating Latin is where piety got sloppy. Many medieval Vulgate copies at 3:15 print *ipsa conteret*: she shall crush. That fed Mary-as-crusher piety — the woman, then the Lord’s mother, as the one who puts her foot on the serpent. Do not rant. Not every Mary doctrine hangs on this one word. Rome has other arguments. This word is not one of them if you are reading Hebrew.

Jerome himself, in *Hebrew Questions on Genesis*, read *ipse*: he. He knew the Hebrew. The Nova Vulgata (the modern Catholic Latin, 1979) prints *ipsum*: the seed, a he/it, not she. Douay-Rheims, translating the older Latin, still says she. The LXX has αὐτός (autos): he. English pew Bibles already have he. ESV, NIV, CSB, KJV, NASB: he. The danger is not that the guys are carrying a she-Bible. The danger is never checking. Someone in the room will have heard “she shall crush” from a grandmother, a medal, a painting, a Latin hymn. You name the Hebrew. You name Jerome. You do not pick a fight with someone’s mother. Then you walk to Genesis 6.

Do not restore this as a student scavenger hunt. They already heard 3:15 last week. Tonight you are the one who remembers that the pronoun was a he, and that English got thinner.

## 3. What the Bible in your hand actually is

Hold one up. That object is four decisions stacked:

**Canon.** Which books are in the covers. Protestant 66 is not the same list as Catholic 73. Orthodox lists vary. Ethiopian lists include 1 Enoch. The guys mostly hold 66. Say so. Do not pretend the other lists are a conspiracy invented last Tuesday.

**Base text.** Which Hebrew or Greek the translators sat on. Almost every English OT in the room sits on the Masoretic Text, specifically the Leningrad Codex via Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia. The NT in ESV/NIV/CSB sits on Nestle-Aland, a critical Greek text. KJV NT sits on the Textus Receptus. Those are not the same Greek pile. Do not lie about that. Also do not make it the night’s lesson.

**Philosophy.** Formal equivalence tries to let the English follow the Hebrew/Greek form (ESV, NASB, KJV, YLT). Dynamic or thought-for-thought tries to carry the sense into natural English (NIV, CSB). Paraphrase retells (The Message). None of these is the source. All of them are downstream of Babel. Every later tongue is thinner than the Hebrew.

**House style.** Crossway’s ESV sits in the KJV–RSV lineage and sounds like it. NIV will say “sons of God” at Genesis 6:2 and still smooth other lines. Some older English and some study notes still flatten heavenly beings into Sethite men. Targums flattened Genesis 6 to sons of nobles. That flattening is a human habit. Do not follow it.

Never treat NIV, ESV, or KJV as equivalent to the Hebrew. Send them back to the words. If two English Bibles disagree, that is a cue to open Hebrew, not a cue to pick a team.

## 4. Which books — Tanakh, Protestant 39, Catholic 73

The Jewish Tanakh is 24 books. The Protestant Old Testament is 39. That is the same material counted differently. Samuel, Kings, and Chronicles each split into two. Ezra and Nehemiah split. The Twelve minor prophets are twelve instead of one. The five Megillot are not a separate bundle. Do not tell the guys “the Jews have a shorter Bible.” They have the same Hebrew Scripture in a different count.

Protestant 66 is 39 Old plus 27 New. That is what most of them hold tonight.

Catholic 73 adds the deuterocanon in the Old Testament. Name the list: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach (Ecclesiasticus), Baruch (with the Letter of Jeremiah), 1 and 2 Maccabees, and the additions to Esther and Daniel (Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three, Susanna, Bel and the Dragon). That is not a smear list. Those books were in the Greek and Latin Bibles the church actually read for centuries.

Do not say Catholics added books in the Middle Ages. They received a longer Greek/Latin Old Testament. The Council of Trent, 1546, defined that list for the Roman church against the Reformation cut. The Reformers went back to the Hebrew canon of the synagogue for the Old Testament. Both moves have a history. Neither move is “they snuck books in in the dark.”

Orthodox lists vary by church. Some include 3 Maccabees, 1 Esdras, Psalm 151, the Prayer of Manasseh. Do not flatten “Orthodox” into one number. If a man in the room is Antiochian or Greek or Russian, you do not need to win his table of contents tonight. You need him to hear Genesis 6.

The 1611 King James printed the Apocrypha between the testaments. It was in the book. It was not called Torah. Later Protestant printings often dropped those pages. If someone’s grandpa had a KJV with extra books in the middle, that is not a scandal. That is 1611.

Label extra books. They cannot overrule Moses. 1 Maccabees is useful history. Wisdom is useful Second Temple piety. 1 Enoch is a Second Temple reading of Genesis 6. None of that is Genesis.

## 5. The New Testament twenty-seven

The 27 were not invented by a late council that woke up and picked winners. They were recognized by use. The churches were already reading the four Gospels, Acts, Paul, and the Catholic epistles as Scripture before anyone printed a table. Athanasius’s Festal Letter 39, AD 367, is the first surviving list that matches our 27 exactly. Hippo (393) and Carthage (397) confirm the same New Testament list in the West.

Edges took longer. Revelation was disputed in some Eastern churches. Hebrews was disputed in some Western churches. 2 Peter, Jude, 2 and 3 John, James sat on the edge in some lists. That is not a secret. It is why Athanasius bothers to list them. Recognition is not the same act as invention.

We do not need a fourth-century bishop to make Paul’s letters true. We also do not sneer at the process. The same church that listed the 27 is the church that read the LXX as its Old Testament in Greek. That is why LXX still matters as a check. It is not why LXX replaces Hebrew.

Do not drag this into a Catholic-versus-Baptist fight. The 27 are shared. The fight, when there is one, is Old Testament deuterocanon and extra-biblical Second Temple books. Tonight the only extra book that will try to barge in is 1 Enoch. You already have a labeled block for that.

## 6. Qumran — two piles

Qumran, Judean desert, roughly 250 BC–70 AD. Finds from 1947 on. Two piles came out of the caves. They are not the same thing.

**Pile A.** Copies of books we already have as Scripture. Hebrew, Aramaic, some Greek. Isaiah, Deuteronomy, Psalms, Genesis, the Twelve. These are about a thousand years older than the medieval Masoretic copies most English Bibles rest on. When pile A and the Masoretic Text agree, the consonants were already stable. For Genesis 6:1–4 the Hebrew is not a DSS-versus-MT fight. Moses stands. The scrolls confirm him. We do not need Qumran to prove Genesis 6. We use it because it is an older witness of the same words.

**Pile B.** The community’s other books. Aramaic Enoch fragments. Genesis Apocryphon 1Q20. Jubilees. Rule texts, war texts, pesharim. How that community read Moses, not Moses. Label EXTRA_BIBLICAL. Do not baptize pile two as Torah. Extra cave books are labeled. They are not Moses.

Pile B still matters without ruling. Those books show how Jews already read Genesis 6 before Jesus: heavenly beings, Watchers, fear of mixed seed. That is why the Lamech/Noah fear in 1Q20 and 1 Enoch 106 only exists if the sons of God are not Sethite men. The SHINING NOAH block on the teacher page already has the story. Do not retell it here. Do not send 1Q20 or 1 Enoch 106 in GroupMe. They cannot overrule Genesis 6:9: Noah is righteous, tamim in his generations, he walked with God.

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church includes 1 Enoch in its broader canon. That is a real church with a real list. We still label 1 Enoch extra-biblical for this study. Jude can allude without turning Enoch into Torah. Canonical stacking is allowed when later Scripture names earlier beings. Enoch cannot overrule Genesis.

If you mention Qumran in the room: two piles. Scripture copies confirm. Extra books labeled. Then sit down.

## 7. Watcher in Scripture is Daniel 4

The word is Aramaic עִיר (ir), plural עִירִין (irin). Root = awake / wakeful. Strong’s H5894. It occurs only in Daniel 4 in the whole Bible. English Bibles (ESV/KJV) print those sentences as 4:13, 4:17, 4:23. Ancient Texts / MT Aramaic numbering prints the same words as 4:10, 4:14, 4:20. Same Watcher. If you open the app to English 4:13 you will miss it. Use 4:10, 4:14, 4:20 in the app.

Those Watchers in Daniel 4 are holy. They execute the Most High’s decree against a proud king. They are not the Genesis 6 rebels.

Genesis 6:1-4 does not say Watcher. It says בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים, sons of God. Stack Job 1:6 and 38:7 for the same phrase. Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4 speak of angels who left their habitation / sinned. They do not use the word Watcher. 1 Enoch uses ἐγρήγοροι / Watchers for the rebels. That is extra-biblical class-language mapped onto Genesis 6. Scripture’s own Watcher-word is Daniel 4, and there they are holy.

Ranks, keep them unmashed:

- Job’s court: sons of God who present themselves. The adversary is among them. They are not the bound ones.
- Genesis 6 / Jude 6 / 2 Peter 2:4: sons of God / angels who left their habitation. Bound. Not Daniel’s holy Watcher.
- Daniel 4: holy Watchers, awake ones, decree against Nebuchadnezzar.

Do not let Enoch baptize Daniel’s holy Watcher as a demon. The Ancient Texts chapter study wrongly treats 1 Enoch as a second Hebrew-Bible Watcher occurrence. It is not. Daniel 4 is the only biblical use of the word. 1 Enoch is extra-biblical. Do not copy that error onto the guys’ page.

## 8. LXX, Targums, and the Masoretic base

**LXX.** The Septuagint is a Jewish Greek translation of Hebrew Scripture, 3rd–2nd century BC, with later books and revisions. The early church’s Old Testament in Greek. Paul and the Gospels often sound like it. It is a check, not a replacement of Hebrew. Some LXX witnesses at Genesis 6:2 — Codex Alexandrinus among them — read angels of God (οἱ ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ) rather than sons of God. That is a translation that already heard heavenly beings. Name it. Do not throw out “sons of God.” The Hebrew stands. The Greek shows how they heard it.

**Targums.** Aramaic synagogue translations, later than the LXX. Targum Onkelos at Genesis 6 sands sons of God to sons of nobles / great men. That is later human flattening. Name it. Do not follow it. The Sethite reading in English pews is the same habit in a different century: take the heavenly phrase and make it safe men.

**Never blanket H430.** Strong’s H430 is אֱלֹהִים (elohim). It is used for the true God, for gods, for divine beings, sometimes for human judges in a handful of disputed texts. If you dump “H430 = God” on every row, you will flatten Psalm 82, flatten Genesis 6, and then act surprised when Job 1:6 has sons of God in the council. Read the phrase in its sentence. בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים in Genesis 6 and Job is the council phrase, not Sethite biology.

**Masoretic Text.** Jewish scribes. Vowels and accents added to the consonants. The Aleppo Codex, about 930 AD, was the prize copy and is now incomplete (most of the Torah portion is gone). The Leningrad Codex, 1008/1009 AD, is the complete medieval copy. Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia is Leningrad in a scholar’s vest. This is the Hebrew base of almost every English Old Testament in the room. The Dead Sea Scrolls’ consonants confirm that base in the main. Where they do not, we say so. Genesis 6:1–4 is agreement. Deuteronomy 32:8 is the fight, and that fight is Week 3.

Peshitta (Syriac) and Vulgate (Latin) are later. Do not make them the spine. Jerome’s Hebrew Questions can still correct a bad Latin copy, as at 3:15. That is a check, not a new source.
