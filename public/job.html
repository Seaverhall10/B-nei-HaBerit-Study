How to read this companion

The narrative paragraphs closely retell Job. Statements marked Study conclusion explain why this study connects Job to the wider covenant story. Use the passage links to check every claim.

I

A man in Uz

There was a man in the land of Uz whose name was Job. He was blameless and upright. He feared Yahweh and turned from evil. Seven sons. Three daughters. Flocks and herds past counting. The greatest of all the people of the East.

His sons would hold a feast in each one’s house, on his day, and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them. When the days of feasting had run their course, Job would send and consecrate them. He would rise early in the morning and offer burnt offerings according to their number. Job said, it may be that my children have sinned and cursed God in their hearts. This Job did continually.

Job 1:1-5

II

The sons of God present themselves

A day came when the sons of God came to present themselves before Yahweh, and the adversary came also among them. Yahweh said to the adversary, from where have you come. The adversary answered, from going to and fro on the earth, and from walking up and down on it.

Yahweh said, have you considered my servant Job, that there is none like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, who fears God and turns from evil. The adversary answered, does Job fear God for nothing. Have you not put a hedge around him, around his house, and around all that he has on every side. You have blessed the work of his hands, and his substance is increased in the land. But stretch out your hand now and touch all that he has, and he will curse you to your face.

Yahweh said to the adversary, behold, all that he has is in your power. Only upon himself do not put forth your hand. So the adversary went out from the presence of Yahweh.

Study conclusionHe does not sneak in. He is there. Permission is not absence. Life is withheld from him.

Job 1:6-12

III

Four messengers

There was a day when his sons and his daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother’s house.

A messenger came to Job. The oxen were plowing, the donkeys feeding beside them. The Sabeans fell on them and took them away. The servants they struck with the edge of the sword. I only am escaped to tell you.

While he was still speaking, another came. The fire of God is fallen from heaven, and has burned up the sheep and the servants, and consumed them. I only am escaped to tell you.

While he was still speaking, another came. The Chaldeans made three bands, and fell upon the camels, and have carried them away, and struck the servants with the edge of the sword. I only am escaped to tell you.

While he was still speaking, another came. Your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking wine in their eldest brother’s house. And behold, a great wind came from across the wilderness, and struck the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young people, and they are dead. I only am escaped to tell you.

Study conclusionNot a wedding. A feast in the house of the firstborn. A wind from the wilderness. The house fell.

Job 1:13-19

IV

Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away

Then Job arose, and tore his robe, and shaved his head, and fell down upon the ground, and worshiped. He said, naked I came from my mother’s womb, and naked I shall return there. Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away. Blessed be the name of Yahweh.

In all this Job did not sin, nor charge God with wrong.

Job 1:20-22

V

Skin for skin

Another day. The sons of God came to present themselves before Yahweh, and the adversary came also among them to present himself before Yahweh. From where have you come. From going to and fro on the earth. Have you considered my servant Job. He still holds fast his integrity, although you moved me against him, to destroy him without cause.

The adversary answered, skin for skin. Yes, all that a man has he will give for his life. Stretch out your hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will curse you to your face.

Yahweh said, behold, he is in your hand. Only spare his life.

So the adversary went out from the presence of Yahweh, and struck Job with sore boils from the sole of his foot to his crown. Job took a potsherd to scrape himself with, and he sat down among the ashes.

Then his wife said to him, do you still hold fast your integrity. Curse God, and die. He said to her, you speak as one of the foolish women speaks. What. Shall we receive good at the hand of God, and shall we not receive evil. In all this Job did not sin with his lips.

Job 2:1-10

VI

Three friends, then a long silence that breaks

When Job’s three friends heard of all this evil that was come upon him, they came every one from his own place. Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite. They had made an appointment together to come to mourn with him and to comfort him. When they lifted up their eyes afar off, and did not know him, they lifted up their voice and wept. They tore every one his robe, and sprinkled dust upon their heads toward heaven. So they sat down with him upon the ground seven days and seven nights, and none spoke a word to him, for they saw that his grief was very great.

After this Job opened his mouth and cursed his day. He did not throw away God. He wanted the pressure to stop. He wished he had never been born.

Then the friends wrecked it. They insisted suffering is always payment for sin, so Job must have sinned. Job maintained his integrity. He wanted a hearing. A fourth man, Elihu, waited because they were older, then spoke of a God who is not on a leash, who speaks in dreams and pain, who is just and beyond finding out.

Job 2:11-13 · Job 3

VII

The whirlwind

Then Yahweh answered Job out of the whirlwind, and said, who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge. Gird up now your loins like a man, for I will demand of you, and you answer me.

Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth. Declare, if you have understanding. Who laid the measures of it, if you know. Or who stretched the line upon it. Whereupon are the foundations thereof fastened. Or who laid the cornerstone thereof, when the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy.

Study conclusionThat is why we opened this book in Week 1. The heavenly family is already there when the world is made. The council is not a later idea. Testing is already in the story. Yahweh remains God.

Job answered Yahweh, I know that you can do everything, and that no thought can be withheld from you. I have heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you. Therefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes.

Job 38:1-7 · Job 42:1-6

VIII

Restored, not explained away

Yahweh said to Eliphaz, my wrath is kindled against you, and against your two friends, for you have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job has. Take seven bulls and seven rams, and go to my servant Job, and offer up for yourselves a burnt offering. My servant Job shall pray for you. For him will I accept.

So Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar did as Yahweh commanded them. Yahweh accepted Job. Yahweh turned the captivity of Job when he prayed for his friends. Yahweh gave Job twice as much as he had before. All his brothers, and all his sisters, and all who had been of his acquaintance before, came to him and ate bread with him in his house, and they bemoaned him, and comforted him over all the evil that Yahweh had brought upon him. Every man also gave him a piece of money, and everyone a ring of gold.

So Yahweh blessed the latter end of Job more than his beginning. Fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, a thousand she-asses. He had also seven sons and three daughters. He called the name of the first Jemimah, the name of the second Keziah, and the name of the third Keren-happuch. In all the land were no women found so fair as the daughters of Job. Their father gave them inheritance among their brothers. After this Job lived a hundred and forty years, and saw his sons, and his sons’ sons, even four generations. So Job died, being old and full of days.

Study conclusionThe first man in the story is not the last Adam. He is the template for a righteous sufferer under a God who is not on a leash.

Job 42:7-17

The whole book in one breath

A righteous man is named in the council. The adversary is already there. Yahweh permits a test and withholds Job’s life. Wave after wave of loss, then a feast in the eldest brother’s house that ends when a wind from the wilderness drops the house on the children. Job worships. A second permission: bone and flesh, not life. Sores. Ash. Friends who sit well and then speak badly. Yahweh answers from the storm: where were you when the morning stars sang and the sons of God shouted. Job sees. Friends must ask Job to pray. Yahweh restores. That is the book. That is why it sits at the front of our map.
